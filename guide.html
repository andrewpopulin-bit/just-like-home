1. What you can change yourself

Your site is hand built, which is why it loads fast and looks the way it does. The trade off is that some things are easy for you to change and some need me. Here is the honest split.

You can do these anytime, no help needed

  • Your availability. Update your booked dates yourself anytime on the My availability page. It is just a Google Sheet, no code.
  • Collecting Google reviews. Send your review link to happy clients anytime, it drops them straight into the review box.
  • New reviews build up on your Google listing automatically, which is what helps you show up when people search. They do not appear on the website itself, that needs a developer.
  • Your Instagram. The site links to your profile, so anything you post shows up when people click through.

These need a web developer

  • Adding or swapping photos
  • Changing prices or service wording
  • Adding or removing suburbs
  • New sections or pages

These live inside the website code. They are small jobs for anyone who builds websites, but not something to attempt yourself unless you are comfortable with code. Section 6 has everything a developer would need to pick it up.

2. Your availability calendar

Your Availability page shows the next three months at a glance, like a hotel. Each date is one of three things:

  • Green, available
  • Amber, limited, one or two spaces left
  • Pink and crossed out, fully booked

It also works on the enquiry form. When someone picks their dates, a note appears telling them whether those dates are free, limited or booked. It never stops them enquiring though, so you still hear from people on busy dates and can pass them to your friends.

Updating it yourself

Your booked dates live in a Google Sheet. You edit the sheet, the website follows a few minutes later. That is the whole system.

  1. Open your My availability page and tap Open my availability sheet.
  2. Add a row: put the dates in the From and To columns, then click the Status cell and pick from the drop-down. There is nothing to type.
  3. That is it. Close the sheet, the site updates itself.

Delete a row once that booking is done. Anything not in the sheet shows as available. You can do all of this from the Google Sheets app on your phone.

You do not need to log every walk

Just block out the stretches you genuinely cannot take work, like your trip to Ireland. A calendar that is roughly right is far more useful than one you feel guilty about keeping perfect.

3. Google reviews

Your Google Business Profile is verified and live, and the Leave a Google review button is on your website. Nothing more to set up.

Your review link

https://g.page/r/Cch0vRdtMHSeEBM/review

Save that in your phone. Send it to clients after a stay and it drops them straight into the review box. Reviews build up on your Google listing, which is what helps you show up when people search for a dog sitter near them.

Two things worth checking in your Google profile settings when you get a minute:

  • Make sure it is set as a service area business listing your suburbs, rather than a shopfront with a street address. Otherwise Google publishes your home address on Maps.
  • Add a few photos and your opening hours. Google favours profiles that look looked after.

Reviews build up on your Google listing rather than on the website. The website has a button pointing people to it, which is the part that matters for showing up in searches.

4. Your domain name

Your website lives at justlikehomebynicole.com. You bought it on GoDaddy, it is registered in your name until 2029, and it is connected and secured with an SSL certificate, which is the padlock you see next to the address.

Nothing to do here. The only thing to remember is that GoDaddy will email you when it is due for renewal, around August 2029. Keep paying that and the address stays yours.

Worth knowing

The domain is yours, not mine. If you ever move to a different website or a different designer, the address moves with you. Nobody can take it off you.

5. Photos, prices and wording

These are the parts written into the website itself, so changing them means editing the code. Any web developer can do it, and none of it is a big job.

Things that fall into this category:

  • Adding, removing or swapping photos
  • Changing your prices or what each service includes
  • Adding or removing suburbs
  • Changing wording anywhere on the site
  • Adding new pages or sections

Keep an eye on your prices

Your prices are printed on the website. If you put them up and the site still shows the old ones, people will hold you to what is written. Worth getting them updated the same week you change them.

6. Everything you own, and where it lives

All of this is in your name. Keep this page somewhere safe, it is everything you or any future web developer would need.

Your accounts

  • Domain: justlikehomebynicole.com, registered with GoDaddy on your account. Renews around August 2029. If that lapses, the website address stops working, so do not ignore the renewal email.
  • Website hosting: GitHub Pages, which is free. There is no monthly bill and nothing to renew.
  • Availability: a Google Sheet on your jlh.petservice account, published to the web so the site can read it.
  • Google Business Profile: your jlh.petservice account. This is what puts you on Google Maps and collects reviews.
  • Enquiries: the form opens the customer's own email app addressed to jlh.petservice@gmail.com, so enquiries land in your normal inbox.

If you hire someone to make changes

Hand them this page. In their language: it is a static HTML, CSS and JavaScript site with no framework and no build step, hosted on GitHub Pages, with availability read from a published Google Sheet CSV. Any web developer will understand that in about a minute.

You will need to give them access to the website files. Whoever set the site up can transfer that to you or to them.

If something looks wrong

  • Site looks out of date? Your phone may be showing a saved copy. Pull down to refresh, or close the tab and open it again.
  • Availability not updating? Give it five minutes, Google holds the published sheet briefly. Check you edited the right sheet and that the dates are in the From and To columns.
  • Whole site down? Almost always the domain renewal. Check your GoDaddy account first.

That is everything. The website, the domain, the Google listing and your availability sheet are all in your name and under your control.
